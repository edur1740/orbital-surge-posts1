ORBITAL SURGE 2027 Game Manual

Fan-made FRC-style game · Kickoff release (Rev P) · 2026-11-11

1 Introduction

This manual is the rulebook for ORBITAL SURGE, a real-world game in the style of the FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC), designed to be played on a physical FIELD by student-built ROBOTS. It sets out the ARENA, how a MATCH is played and scored, and the rules for DRIVE TEAMS and ROBOTS. ORBITAL SURGE is not affiliated with or endorsed by FIRST.

1.1 Companion materials

The Kickoff release site holds the game animation, the one-page Game Overview, Field Drawings (OS-FD-001 field layout, OS-FD-002 element details, OS-FD-003 ROBOT measurement), the Plasma Cell specification, Team Update 00, and the Kit of Parts checklist. Where this manual and a drawing disagree, this manual controls.

1.2 Conventions

  • Defined terms appear in ALL CAPS and are listed in Section 8, Glossary.

  • Rule IDs are a letter plus three or four digits. The letter is the section (G = Game, R = Robot, I = Inspection, T = Tournament), the digits before the last two are the rule group, numbered in the order the groups appear (R101 is the first rule of group 1, R1001 the first of group 10), and the last two digits are the rule's place in it. Example: G402.

  • Each rule has a short bold headline. The rule text controls over the headline.

  • Rules marked (E) are evergreen: they carry over from season to season with little change.

  • Blue-box notes explain intent. They do not carry the weight of a rule.

  • Dimensions are in inches, with metric in parentheses where it matters. The imperial value controls.

  • Violations are named in ALL CAPS: VERBAL WARNING, MINOR FOUL, MAJOR FOUL, YELLOW CARD, RED CARD, DISABLED, DISQUALIFIED. They are assessed per instance unless the rule says otherwise.

    Times are shown as the MATCH timer counts down. "0:00" on its own always means the end of the MATCH; the end of the AUTO period is always called "the end of AUTO".

The Q&A opens the Wednesday after Kickoff at noon Eastern. Each question must cite the specific rule or drawing it asks about. Answers clarify existing rules; they never create new ones. Q&A answers do not bind the Head REFEREE at an event, but a Team Update does.

2 Game Overview

In ORBITAL SURGE, two ALLIANCES restart a dying space station. They launch PLASMA CELLS into a shared REACTOR CORE, build their own REACTOR ARRAY from FUEL RODS that their HUMAN PLAYERS push through an AIRLOCK, trigger SURGES, ride out a METEOR SHOWER, and climb their TETHER TOWER before time runs out.

The story. Orbital station HELIOS-7 has gone dark. The reactor still holds a spark, but it needs plasma, and each alliance's power array has been stripped of fuel rods. Supply crews can only push new rods through airlocks on the far side of the station, so every rod crosses the deck. When the reactor charges, crews dump the energy into a SURGE that doubles every rod placed. Halfway through, the station drifts into a meteor stream. At the end, robots haul themselves up the tether towers.

A MATCH in one paragraph. Each MATCH lasts 2:30: 0:15 of AUTO, when ROBOTS run without driver input, then 2:15 of TELEOP. At 1:00 the METEOR SHOWER drops 12 extra CELLS. The last 0:25 is the ENDGAME, when ROBOTS climb. Two kinds of scoring run all match long and pull robots in different directions. CELLS go into the CORE at center field. RODS come from the far end of the field and go to the ARRAY at your own wall.

Ways to score

  • MOBILITY: fully cross your STARTING LINE during AUTO.

  • Launch CELLS into the REACTOR CORE. Every 15 CELLS charges a SURGE.

  • Place FUEL RODS on your REACTOR ARRAY: higher levels are worth more, and three side by side on one level form a LINK.

  • Trigger a SURGE so that RODS placed in the next 12 seconds score double.

  • Climb your TETHER TOWER to LOW, MID or HIGH, or PARK in its zone.

  • Earn CHAIN, CHARGE and ASCENT bonus Ranking Points.

3 ARENA

The FIELD is a 640.0 in × 328.0 in (16.26 m × 8.33 m) carpeted deck enclosed by 20 in guardrails and an ALLIANCE WALL at each end. It holds one shared REACTOR CORE, two PYLONS, and for each ALLIANCE a REACTOR ARRAY, a TETHER TOWER and an AIRLOCK. Field Drawings OS-FD-001 and OS-FD-002 (Rev E) show the layout and element details; OS-FD-003 shows how ROBOTS are measured.

3.1 Dimensions and accuracy

Fields are built to drawing, but venues vary. Design for ±1.0 in on any dimension. Marking tape is 2 in wide and dimensions are to tape center. The field has 180° rotational symmetry: every RED element mirrors a BLUE one.

3.3 REACTOR CORE

One CORE sits at center field: a 57.6 in cylinder with an open rim 72.0 in off the carpet. A 24 in tall clear divider splits the rim into a RED half, facing the RED end, and a BLUE half. A CELL that drops through the rim is SCORED for the ALLIANCE whose half it enters, then VENTED back onto the FIELD 2–4 seconds later. Rim lights are cyan normally and glow alliance color during that ALLIANCE's SURGE.

Inside, each half funnels into its own chute past two beam-break sensors, so the FMS counts every CELL even when several drop at once. The chutes feed four exit ramps at the base, one facing each corner of the FIELD, that roll CELLS back onto the carpet. The CORE shell is 1/8 in polycarbonate on an aluminum frame, bolted to the carpet so a 160 lb ROBOT at full speed cannot shift it (a legal ROBOT weighs at most 154 lb with BUMPERS and battery).

3.4 PYLONS

Two 25.6 in × 72.0 in PYLONS stand on the CENTER LINE, 50.0 in from each guardrail, splitting the field into three driving lanes. Each PYLON carries a METEOR HOPPER on top that holds 6 CELLS behind an FMS-driven gate. At 1:00 both gates open and the CELLS roll down two ramps to the carpet, one on each side of the PYLON, so each PYLON feeds both the middle lane and its side lane.

3.5 REACTOR ARRAYS

Each ALLIANCE's ARRAY is a rack against its own ALLIANCE WALL: 96.0 in wide, 16.0 in deep and 66.0 in tall. It has 6 SLOTS at a 16.0 in pitch on each of 3 LEVELS: L1 (18 in), L2 (36 in) and L3 (54 in), 18 positions in all. A ROD is PLACED when it rests fully in a slot. Each slot has a light that turns on when it holds a ROD.

3.6 TETHER TOWERS

Each ALLIANCE has one TOWER along the far guardrail (RED) or table-side guardrail (BLUE) in its own end: a 88.0 × 22.4 in truss, 78.0 in tall, hanging three TETHERS. Each TETHER ends in a 1.25 in round grab bar. A ROBOT CLIMBS by lifting until its BUMPERS clear the carpet by 6 in (LOW), 18 in (MID) or 30 in (HIGH).

Each TETHER is 3/8 in steel cable in a vinyl sleeve, rated for a 300 lb working load, with its grab bar 66 in off the carpet. The truss is 2 in square steel tube, weighted at the base so it cannot tip. Only one ROBOT may hang from each TETHER.

3.7 AIRLOCKS and HUMAN PLAYER stations

Each ALLIANCE's HUMAN PLAYER stands at the opponent's end, behind a chute that opens into its AIRLOCK ZONE. The HUMAN PLAYER holds up to 14 FUEL RODS (fewer when some are preloaded, 4.2) and feeds one at a time; the next can't be fed until the last has fully passed the chute exit. A SURGE button sits beside each chute.

An ALLIANCE's AIRLOCK is the one its own HUMAN PLAYER feeds. It sits at the opponent's end, so "RED's AIRLOCK" is physically on the BLUE side of the FIELD.

4 Game Details

A MATCH is two ALLIANCES of three ROBOTS playing for 2:30. Points come from MOBILITY, CELLS in the CORE, RODS and LINKS on the ARRAY, and TETHER climbs, plus any FOUL points an opponent gives up.

4.1 DRIVE TEAM

Each DRIVE TEAM has up to five people: two DRIVERS, one HUMAN PLAYER, one DRIVE COACH and one TECHNICIAN. Each team may bring a HUMAN PLAYER, but each ALLIANCE picks one before the MATCH to staff its AIRLOCK chute at the opponent's end. The others stay in the ALLIANCE STATION. That person feeds RODS and presses the SURGE button, and can't be swapped out during a MATCH. In playoffs the HUMAN PLAYER must come from one of the 3 teams playing that MATCH. A card for the HUMAN PLAYER's actions goes to the team that person belongs to.

4.2 Setup

  1. Each ROBOT starts fully behind its STARTING LINE, touching the carpet.

  2. Each ROBOT is preloaded with either 1 CELL or 1 ROD, the team's choice. A preloaded ROD comes out of its ALLIANCE's 14 AIRLOCK RODS; FIELD STAFF place each unused preload CELL on its ALLIANCE's STARTING LINE, starting 24 in from the scoring-table guardrail and spaced 24 in apart, never touching a ROBOT. Teams don't choose the spot.

  3. Staff stage 18 CELLS (8 on a ring 86 in from the CORE, two columns of 4, one 112 in each side of center, 1 between each PYLON and the CORE) and 4 RODS near the guardrails, 80 in either side of the CENTER LINE.

  4. Each HUMAN PLAYER loads its ALLIANCE's remaining RODS into the AIRLOCK chute: 14 minus one for each of its ALLIANCE's ROBOTS that preloads a ROD. For example, if one ROBOT preloads a ROD, the chute gets 13. Teams declare their preloads to FIELD STAFF before ROBOTS are set; FIELD STAFF count each chute before the MATCH starts. A wrong count found before the start is fixed then. If found after, the points from any extra RODS are removed, starting with the lowest-value RODS, and those RODS don't count toward LINKS. It is also a MAJOR FOUL against that ALLIANCE, but only if its own HUMAN PLAYER loaded the wrong number or its teams declared preloads wrongly, not if FIELD STAFF miscounted. If RODS are missing, FIELD STAFF hand them to the HUMAN PLAYER as soon as it is found.

4.4 SURGE

Each ALLIANCE has a SURGE meter that fills by one for every CELL SCORED in its half of the CORE in TELEOP while it is not surging, whoever launched it (G418 covers CELLS put there on purpose). At 15 the meter is full: the SURGE button lights, and further CELLS don't add to it until it is used. When the HUMAN PLAYER presses it, the ALLIANCE SURGES for 12 seconds: every ROD it places in that time scores double. The CORE rim glows that ALLIANCE's color, and the meter drops to 0. CELLS scored while surging don't fill the meter. A SURGE can't be saved past the end of the MATCH. LINK bonuses are not doubled.

SURGE timing. A ROD scores double only if it is PLACED while the SURGE is active and was not touching any ARRAY slot when the SURGE started. A ROD already resting partly in a slot, or touching one, when the SURGE starts is never doubled, even if it is pushed fully in later. A ROD knocked out and placed again is judged by its latest PLACE, under the same test. The FMS timestamp sets when a ROD became PLACED; referees decide whether it was touching a slot when the SURGE started.

SURGE examples. A meter at 15 lights the button, and the next 3 CELLS scored add nothing. Pressing it starts a SURGE and drops the meter to 0. CELLS scored during those 12 seconds don't add to it, so it stays at 0 until the SURGE ends. Only one SURGE per ALLIANCE can run at a time.

Note: Timing a SURGE is the heart of the strategy. Have a ROD in hand, and ideally two ROBOTS lined up at the ARRAY, before you press the button.

4.5 Scoring criteria

  • A CELL is SCORED when it drops fully through the CORE rim. It counts for the ALLIANCE whose half of the rim it enters (3.3). A VENTED CELL is back in play and may be SCORED again.

  • A ROD is PLACED when it rests fully in an ARRAY slot and no ROBOT is touching it. When the ARRAY is read after 0:00, a ROD resting fully in a slot counts even if an opponent ROBOT is touching it. Each slot holds one ROD; a ROD resting across two slots, or only partly in one, scores nothing. ROD points are counted from the ARRAY as it stands at 0:00: each ROD in a slot scores once, at its LEVEL's value. It earns the AUTO value only if it was PLACED in AUTO and never left its slot, and it is doubled only if its latest PLACE met the SURGE timing test (4.4). Taking a PLACED ROD out of any ARRAY, including your own, is not allowed (G403). If a PLACED ROD leaves its slot because of an opponent's violation, or is knocked out by an opponent ROBOT or a CELL an opponent launched, it still scores in the slot it left, at its original value and SURGE status, and that slot counts as filled, for points and LINKS, for the rest of the MATCH. No other ROD can score in that slot, and the displaced ROD scores nothing if it is PLACED again. If its own ALLIANCE removes it, it scores nothing (G403). If it leaves for any other reason, it scores only if it is PLACED again by 0:00, at the value of its new slot, in TELEOP, using its new PLACE time for SURGE. The FMS logs each slot's PLACE and clear times, and referees note any displaced ROD and why it moved.

  • A LINK is three RODS in adjacent slots on the same LEVEL. Six in a row is two LINKS. Each ROD can count toward only one LINK. To count LINKS, look at each LEVEL's runs of filled, side-by-side slots: a run of 3, 4 or 5 RODS is one LINK and a run of 6 is two. LINKS are counted from the ARRAY at 0:00 and are worth 5 each, whenever their RODS were PLACED. A ROD that scores nothing (G403, G417, 4.2, or a displaced ROD PLACED again) does not count toward LINKS, and its slot counts as empty. A slot credited under G430 or kept filled under 4.5 counts as filled.

  • A ROBOT has MOBILITY if its BUMPERS fully cross its STARTING LINE during AUTO under its own power; being pushed across doesn't count.

  • A ROBOT is PARKED if its BUMPERS are partly inside its own TOWER ZONE at 0:00 and it is not CLIMBED at any LEVEL, including a ROBOT that tried to climb but didn't reach LOW. A ROBOT scores PARK or a CLIMB, never both.

  • A ROBOT is CLIMBED at a LEVEL if, at 0:00, it hangs from one of its own TETHERS with the lowest point of its BUMPERS above that LEVEL's height, and is held up only by that TETHER: it touches no carpet, TOWER truss or other FIELD element, and no other ROBOT carries any of its weight. Light contact with a partner ROBOT that isn't holding it up is fine. A ROBOT whose BUMPERS are not in their inspected R401 position scores no CLIMB.

Sensors and referees. The FMS count is the score unless a referee saw it was wrong. Before the Head REFEREE confirms the score, referees fix both kinds of sensor error the same way. A missed count is added: a CELL that dropped through the rim but wasn't counted, or a ROD resting fully in a slot whose light stayed off. A false count is removed: a CELL counted that never went through, or a slot reading PLACED with no ROD fully in it. For RODS, the ROD's physical position decides: a referee checks any disputed slot before the FIELD is reset, and the slot light does not override what the referee sees. SURGE timing follows 4.4. A DRIVE TEAM's student designee may ask for a check before confirmation (G502). After confirmation the score is final.

Timing at the end of a period. A CELL counts for AUTO if it was launched before AUTO ends and drops through the rim within 3 seconds after. A ROD counts as PLACED in AUTO if it was released before AUTO ends and comes to rest in its slot within 3 seconds after. At the end of the MATCH, CELLS launched before 0:00 that drop through the rim within 5 seconds still count. The FMS disables every ROBOT at 0:00; the ARRAY and climbs are read once pieces and ROBOTS settle, at most 5 seconds later. RODS released after 0:00 don't count.

Pieces out of play. A CELL or ROD that leaves the FIELD, or lodges on top of a FIELD element where ROBOTS can't reach it, is out for the rest of the MATCH; FIELD STAFF don't return it. A ROBOT that lifts, pushes or places a piece off the FIELD or onto a FIELD element on purpose commits a MAJOR FOUL (G437), and FIELD STAFF return that piece to the FIELD at the nearest guardrail when it is safe. A cracked ROD stays in play unless the Head REFEREE judges it unsafe.

4.8 Violations

A MINOR FOUL credits 5 points to the opponent and a MAJOR FOUL credits 15. One action gets one penalty: when an action breaks more than one rule, only the most severe penalty applies, unless a rule says its penalty comes "on top of" another. A climb credited by a FOUL is capped at the highest LEVEL that ROBOT has reached at the event, including its I106 inspection test. Two YELLOW CARDS make a RED CARD, and a RED CARD DISQUALIFIES the team from that MATCH. In qualification MATCHES a DISQUALIFIED team gets 0 Ranking Points for that MATCH, while its partners keep the ALLIANCE's score and RP. In playoffs the whole ALLIANCE is DISQUALIFIED and loses the MATCH. In playoffs, cards apply to the whole ALLIANCE. A YELLOW CARD carries through the rest of qualifications: a team's second YELLOW CARD, in any later qualification MATCH, becomes a RED CARD. Cards reset when playoffs begin (T206). In playoffs an ALLIANCE's second YELLOW CARD, from any of its teams, becomes a RED CARD. A DISABLED ROBOT is stopped by the FMS for the rest of the MATCH; it keeps points it already scored and can still count for PARK or CLIMB from where it is at 0:00.

5 Game Rules (G)

These rules govern DRIVE TEAMS and ROBOTS from the moment they enter the FIELD until the MATCH is over and the FIELD is reset.

5.0 General principles (evergreen)

These rules apply every season and sit above every other rule in Section 5. When another rule seems to allow something these forbid, these win.

G001 Safety first. Any action a referee sees as unsafe to people, ROBOTS or the FIELD may be penalized even if no other rule names it. The Head REF may DISABLE a ROBOT that is unsafe, on fire, smoking, or shedding parts. Violation: MAJOR FOUL, plus YELLOW CARD if repeated or deliberate.

G002 Small slips are not punished. A violation that is both accidental and has no effect on the MATCH is not penalized, unless it breaks a safety rule. Referees decide what counts as accidental and as having no effect.

G003 Egregious behavior. Behavior that is extreme, deliberate, or clearly against the spirit of the game may earn a RED CARD, even if it is a first offense.

G004 Repeats escalate. If a team keeps breaking the same rule in a MATCH, referees may step the penalty up: MINOR to MAJOR, then YELLOW CARD, then RED CARD.

G005 Referees judge intent. Where a rule depends on intent, referees decide it from what they see on the FIELD. A team's explanation afterward does not change the call.

G006 No baiting fouls. A ROBOT may not drive, extend, or position itself to force an opponent into a foul. A foul caused this way is not given to the opponent, and the baiting ROBOT gets the foul instead. Violation: MAJOR FOUL.

G007 Questions go through one student. Questions follow G502: one student from the DRIVE TEAM (the team's student designee) may question a call, only with the Head REF, at the question box, and before the next MATCH that team plays in starts. Arguing with referees on the FIELD, or sending other team members to do it, is a G003 violation. When a score can still change is set by G502.

G008 No fouling as a strategy. A team may not commit FOULS on purpose, or accept them as the price of a bonus, such as carrying extra CELLS to reach the CHARGE RP or fouling freely once it has nothing left to play for. Violation: YELLOW CARD on top of the FOULS; in qualifications the Head REF may also deny any bonus RP the violations helped earn.

5.1 Personal safety

G101 (E) Wear safety glasses. Everyone on the FIELD, in an ALLIANCE STATION or at a HUMAN PLAYER station must wear ANSI Z87.1-rated safety glasses. Violation: VERBAL WARNING, then MINOR FOUL per further instance.

G102 (E) Stay out of the FIELD during a MATCH. DRIVE TEAM members may not step onto the carpet or reach into the FIELD while the MATCH is running. Violation: MAJOR FOUL and YELLOW CARD.

5.2 Conduct

G201 (E) Be a good sport. Teams must show Gracious Professionalism: treat opponents, volunteers and other teams with respect. Violation: YELLOW or RED CARD.

G202 (E) Play to win. Teams may not intentionally lose a MATCH or give up RP to manipulate rankings. ALLIANCES may not agree with each other to fix a result or to skip defense or scoring. Violation: RED CARD.

G205 Strategy sheets allowed. The DRIVE COACH may hold one printed strategy sheet in the ALLIANCE STATION. No electronics other than the OPERATOR CONSOLE in the ALLIANCE STATION, and none other than the SURGE button at the HUMAN PLAYER station.

5.3 Pre-MATCH

G301 Start in position. Each ROBOT must start fully behind its STARTING LINE with exactly 1 preloaded CELL or ROD. Violation: the MATCH does not start until fixed; if not fixed promptly, the ROBOT is DISABLED.

G302 (E) Stay inside the starting size. At MATCH start a ROBOT may not be taller than 42 in, extend beyond its FRAME PERIMETER (BUMPERS excepted), or fall outside the R109 starting box. Violation: the ROBOT is DISABLED.

G303 Start clear of their areas. No part of a ROBOT may start inside, or touching the tape of, an opponent's AIRLOCK ZONE, AIRLOCK APPROACH, ARRAY APPROACH or TOWER APPROACH. Violation: the MATCH does not start until it's fixed.

G304 Right BUMPERS. A ROBOT must start with BUMPERS of its ALLIANCE's color, fully attached. Violation: the MATCH does not start until it's fixed; if it can't be fixed promptly, the ROBOT is bypassed (T207).

G305 (E) Don't stall the start. A DRIVE TEAM may not delay the MATCH start on purpose. Violation: VERBAL WARNING, then YELLOW CARD; a ROBOT still not ready is bypassed (T207).

5.4 In-MATCH

G401 Piece limits. A ROBOT may CONTROL at most 2 CELLS and 1 ROD at once. CELLS that land on a ROBOT on their own (from the METEOR SHOWER, a VENT or an opponent's launch) don't count toward the limit for 3 seconds while it sheds them, as long as it launches no CELL in that time. CELLS a partner launches or passes to it count at once. The count starts when the ROBOT first goes over the limit, keeps running while it stays over, and resets once it has been at or under the limit for 3 seconds. Each reset starts a new instance: the MINOR FOUL per extra piece is charged once per instance, and the repeating MAJOR FOULS run off the same count. Violation: MINOR FOUL per extra piece; MAJOR FOUL if it lasts more than 3 seconds, plus 1 more MAJOR FOUL for each further 3 seconds.

G402 Stay out of their AIRLOCK. A ROBOT may not enter the opponent's AIRLOCK ZONE or take a ROD from it. One exception: an opponent ROBOT may reach in for up to 3 seconds to take CELLS out, touching no RODS. Violation: MINOR FOUL, repeated every 2 seconds the ROBOT stays.

G403 Hands off their ARRAY. A ROBOT may not touch the opponent's REACTOR ARRAY, including any ROD in its slots, or remove a PLACED ROD from either ARRAY. Violation: MAJOR FOUL and YELLOW CARD. A ROD an opponent removes still counts as PLACED in the slot it left. A ROD a ROBOT removes from its own ALLIANCE's ARRAY scores nothing for the rest of the MATCH, even if PLACED again.

G404 Leave a climbing opponent alone. During the ENDGAME, a ROBOT may not contact an opponent ROBOT whose BUMPERS are at least partly inside that opponent's own TOWER ZONE and that is climbing or getting into position to climb. A ROBOT playing defense from inside its own TOWER ZONE is not protected. Violation: MAJOR FOUL per contact; if the contact stops a climb, the blocked ROBOT is credited with the CLIMB LEVEL the referee judges it was going for, or PARK if it had not started a climb.

G405 (E) No pinning. A ROBOT may not pin an opponent for more than 3 seconds. A pin starts when a ROBOT holds an opponent against a FIELD element or another ROBOT so it can't drive away. It ends when the pinned ROBOT is free to drive away, or the pinning ROBOT backs off at least 3 ft. After a pin ends, no opponent may pin that same ROBOT again for 3 seconds; a pin by a partner of the first pinning ROBOT within that time continues the same count. Violation: MINOR FOUL, plus 1 more for each further 3 seconds.

G406 Launch only into the CORE. CELLS may only be launched toward the CORE. A launch is any CELL the ROBOT releases so that it moves away from the ROBOT, whether through the air, rolling or dropped. Setting a CELL down at rest is not a launch. It is away from the CORE if it leaves the ROBOT more than 45° off the line from the ROBOT to the CORE's center. A launch away from the CORE is deliberate if the launcher was aimed that way when it fired, or if it passes the CELL to a partner or a spot or is meant to carry over the CORE, even within 45°. Repeated launches away from the CORE are evidence of intent that referees weigh with everything else they see, not proof on their own. A launch caused by a jam, a hit from another ROBOT or a mechanism failure is accidental however often it happens; a ROBOT that keeps misfiring that way may be DISABLED for safety instead of fouled. Violation: MINOR FOUL per CELL launched away from the CORE or out of the FIELD; MAJOR FOUL per CELL if it was deliberate, whether or not it leaves the FIELD.

G407 HUMAN PLAYERS feed, they don't throw. HUMAN PLAYERS may only put RODS into play through their AIRLOCK chute, one at a time, without their hands passing the chute exit, and may press the SURGE button only while it is lit. Violation: MINOR FOUL per ROD; a SURGE pressed early does nothing.

G408 No AUTO interference. During AUTO, a ROBOT may not cross the CENTER LINE far enough to contact an opponent ROBOT on the opponent's side, or CONTROL a CELL or ROD that started fully on the opponent's side (MAJOR FOUL per piece). Violation: MAJOR FOUL per contact.

G409 (E) No tipping or entanglement. A ROBOT may not tip, entangle or damage an opponent deliberately or recklessly. Violation: MAJOR FOUL and YELLOW CARD.

G410 Launch from the LAUNCH ZONE. A ROBOT may only launch CELLS while its BUMPERS are at least partly inside the LAUNCH ZONE. Violation: MINOR FOUL per CELL.

G411 One ROBOT per TETHER, your own only. A ROBOT may only CLIMB its own ALLIANCE's TETHERS, and may not grab a TETHER another ROBOT is already hanging from. At no time in the MATCH may a ROBOT touch the opponent's TETHERS or TOWER. Violation: MAJOR FOUL; the offending ROBOT's climb does not count.

G412 (E) Don't damage the FIELD. A ROBOT may not damage, bend or detach FIELD elements, including the CORE divider, ARRAY slots, TETHER sleeves and METEOR HOPPER ramps. Violation: MAJOR FOUL, plus YELLOW CARD if the damage is deliberate or repeated; the ROBOT is DISABLED if it keeps causing damage (see also G439).

G413 Stay off the structures. A ROBOT may not climb onto, lift or grab the CORE, a PYLON, a METEOR HOPPER or any ARRAY. Pushing a piece into a slot is fine; hanging on the rack is not. No part of a ROBOT may cover, hover over or reach across the CORE rim's opening. Violation: MAJOR FOUL.

G414 Hold the climb. A CLIMBED ROBOT must stay where it is, without power, until the Head REFEREE confirms the score. FIELD STAFF then lower it. Violation: a ROBOT that slips below its LEVEL before confirmation scores the LEVEL it ends at.

G415 (E) Shut down on command. DRIVE TEAMS must stop their ROBOT at once when the FMS or a REFEREE calls an E-STOP, and may not restart it during that MATCH. Violation: RED CARD.

G416 Stay out of their TOWER ZONE in the ENDGAME. During the ENDGAME, no part of a ROBOT, BUMPERS or mechanisms, may be inside the opponent's TOWER ZONE. Crossing through in under 3 seconds without blocking anyone is fine. Time here adds to the shared G427 count. Violation: MAJOR FOUL, plus 1 more per further 3 seconds; if it blocks a climb, the blocked opponent ROBOT is credited with the CLIMB LEVEL the referee judges it was going for, or PARK if it had not started a climb.

G417 Who owns the RODS. RODS fed through an AIRLOCK chute belong to that ALLIANCE from the moment they enter the chute until they are PLACED or the MATCH ends, wherever they are on the FIELD. An opponent may not CONTROL an owned ROD; pushing one aside while driving through is fine. Only the 4 staged RODS are neutral, and either ALLIANCE may take them. A ROD or CELL a ROBOT is CONTROLLING may not be taken from it, or knocked loose on purpose so it can be taken. Violation: MAJOR FOUL; a piece taken this way can't score for the ALLIANCE that took it.

G418 Don't score for the other side. A ROBOT may not launch, push or otherwise direct CELLS into the opponent's half of the CORE on purpose. Referees judge intent from what they see: a launcher pointed at the opponent's half, a ROBOT launching from the opponent's side of the divider, or a pattern. Repeated CELLS from the same ROBOT entering the opponent's half are evidence of intent, not proof on their own. A ROBOT clearly aiming at its own half is not in violation, however often it misses. A CELL that bounces in off a ROBOT, a PYLON, the rim or the divider is never a violation. Violation: MAJOR FOUL and YELLOW CARD; the CELL still counts for the half it entered.

G419 Don't block their AIRLOCK. The AIRLOCK APPROACH is the 40 in × 40 in square next to each AIRLOCK ZONE, on its CENTER LINE side. A ROBOT may not stay in the opponent's AIRLOCK APPROACH for more than 3 seconds or BLOCK an opponent ROBOT from reaching its AIRLOCK ZONE. Opponents can't enter the AIRLOCK ZONE at all (G402), so the chute exit is already protected. BLOCKING (Glossary) applies here from any direction while any part of the blocking ROBOT, BUMPERS included, is within 60 in of the AIRLOCK ZONE tape. Time in the AIRLOCK APPROACH adds to the shared G427 count. Violation: MINOR FOUL, repeated every 3 seconds.

G420 Don't wall off their TOWER. The TOWER APPROACH is the 40 in deep strip across the full width of each TOWER ZONE, on its CENTER LINE side. During the ENDGAME, a ROBOT may not spend more than 3 seconds in the opponent's TOWER APPROACH and TOWER ZONE combined. Time in either area adds to the shared G427 count. A ROBOT also may not BLOCK an opponent's route into its TOWER ZONE from any direction, including along the TOWER's inner side, while any part of the blocking ROBOT is within 60 in of the TOWER ZONE tape (BLOCKING is defined in the Glossary). It applies whether or not a climb has started. Violation: MAJOR FOUL, plus 1 more MAJOR FOUL for each further 3 seconds the ROBOT stays or keeps BLOCKING; if it stops or prevents a climb, the blocked opponent ROBOT is credited with the CLIMB LEVEL the referee judges it was going for, or PARK if it had not started a climb.

G421 Keep the CORE flowing. No part of a ROBOT may stay within 12 in of a CORE exit ramp's opening for more than 3 seconds, stop CELLS from rolling out of it, or push pieces into it. The same applies to the bottom of each METEOR HOPPER ramp from 1:05 until its CELLS have rolled off. CELLS count only when they drop through the rim, never when pushed in through an exit. Each ROBOT has its own count here, separate from G427: it starts when any part of the ROBOT is within 12 in and resets after 3 seconds away. Violation: MINOR FOUL, repeated every 3 seconds; MAJOR FOUL if CELLS back up inside the CORE.

G422 Don't park on their ARRAY. The ARRAY APPROACH is the 24 in deep strip across the full width of each ARRAY, in front of its face. A ROBOT may not spend more than 3 seconds in the opponent's ARRAY APPROACH (count per G427), or BLOCK an opponent from reaching its ARRAY APPROACH, using the same BLOCKING test as G419. Violation: MINOR FOUL, repeated every 3 seconds.

G423 Don't throw RODS. A ROBOT may not launch a ROD. A ROD may leave a ROBOT only by being PLACED in a slot, set down, or dropped from no more than 12 in. Violation: MINOR FOUL per ROD; MAJOR FOUL and YELLOW CARD if it flies more than 3 ft or toward a person.

G424 (E) No fishing for fouls. A ROBOT that makes contact on purpose, or drives into an opponent's path, to draw a FOUL doesn't earn that FOUL. Violation: as G006, a MAJOR FOUL against the baiting ROBOT.

G425 (E) BUMPERS and loose parts. A ROBOT whose BUMPERS fall off, or no longer cover 75% of its FRAME PERIMETER in the 2–7 in zone, must stop contacting other ROBOTS; the Head REFEREE may DISABLE it. Parts that come off a ROBOT are not game pieces and may not be used. Violation: MAJOR FOUL per contact while in that state.

G426 Climb only in the ENDGAME. A ROBOT may not grab or hang from a TETHER before the ENDGAME starts. Violation: MAJOR FOUL; that ROBOT can't score a CLIMB in that MATCH.

G427 Protected areas. An opponent's protected areas are its AIRLOCK APPROACH and ARRAY APPROACH, plus, in the ENDGAME, its TOWER APPROACH and TOWER ZONE. Each ROBOT has its own count. A ROBOT is in an area as soon as any part of it, BUMPERS or mechanisms, is over that area's tape. Time spent in any of one opponent's protected areas adds to a single 3-second count, which resets only after the ROBOT has been fully out of all of them for 3 seconds. When the count passes 3 seconds, the penalty is the most severe one among the areas the ROBOT has been in during that count (G416, G419, G420 or G422). Inside them, contact must be incidental. An opponent is protected from contact only while its BUMPERS are at least partly inside the area and it is scoring, feeding or climbing there, not while it plays defense. Violation: pushing or holding a protected opponent inside its protected area is a MAJOR FOUL per contact, on top of any count violation.

G428 (E) Forced violations. A ROBOT pushed into a violation by an opponent, including into a protected area, isn't penalized and the time doesn't count, as long as it tries to get out right away. A ROBOT that uses contact to force an opponent into a violation earns nothing from it (G424).

G429 (E) Hands off in AUTO. During AUTO, no DRIVE TEAM member may touch the OPERATOR CONSOLE or signal the ROBOT in any way (signs, lights or sounds aimed at its sensors), except to E-STOP. Violation: MAJOR FOUL; that ROBOT's AUTO points don't count.

G430 Don't jam their ARRAY. A ROBOT may not put a CELL or ROD into or onto the opponent's ARRAY, by touch or by launching. Violation: MAJOR FOUL and YELLOW CARD if deliberate, MINOR FOUL if not. Either way, each slot blocked scores for the opponent as if it held a ROD at that slot's TELEOP value, and counts toward their LINKS. A credited slot is never doubled by a SURGE, and a jam an opponent forced (G428) earns that opponent no credit. A launched CELL that hits the opponent's ARRAY is a MINOR FOUL even if it doesn't stay, or a MAJOR FOUL if deliberate.

G431 Stay legal during the MATCH. A ROBOT must stay within R103, R106, R107 and R401 the whole MATCH. Violation: MINOR FOUL per instance; MAJOR FOUL if it scores, blocks or defends while over a limit.

G432 (E) Don't leave parts behind. A ROBOT may not drop, release or leave parts on the FIELD on purpose. Violation: MAJOR FOUL and YELLOW CARD.

G433 No stashing in an AIRLOCK. A ROBOT may not leave CELLS or RODS in any AIRLOCK ZONE on purpose, other than RODS just fed there through the chute. Driving through with pieces it is CONTROLLING is fine. The penalty applies per piece left. Violation: MINOR FOUL per CELL.

G434 Stay out of their frame. A ROBOT may not reach inside an opponent's FRAME PERIMETER with any mechanism that is outside its own FRAME PERIMETER. This does not apply if the opponent's own mechanism is also outside its frame at that spot and the contact happens there. Violation: MINOR FOUL per contact. MAJOR FOUL plus YELLOW CARD if it damages or disables the opponent.

G435 Repeat damage. A ROBOT that damages opponents in two MATCHES at one event is DISABLED until it passes re-inspection (I109). A third time removes it from the event's remaining MATCHES. Damage a ROBOT suffers because its own part broke R204 or stuck outside its FRAME PERIMETER does not count against the other ROBOT.

G436 One piece, no anchors. A ROBOT may not split into separate parts, connect itself to anything with cables or lines, or grip, puncture or anchor to the carpet or any FIELD element. Hanging from its own TETHER in the ENDGAME (G411, R1101) is the one allowed grip. Dropping parts is covered by G432. Violation: MAJOR FOUL, and the ROBOT is DISABLED if a dropped part is a hazard.

G437 Game pieces and FIELD for their purpose. CELLS and RODS may not be changed (taped, bent, marked, or damaged on purpose) or used as tools to push, wedge, or block, and may not be removed from play on purpose (4.5). FIELD elements may not be used in ways this manual doesn't intend, such as wedging against, launching off or hiding pieces on them; driving along or bracing against guardrails, walls and the CORE base is fine. Violation: MAJOR FOUL, plus YELLOW CARD if a game piece or FIELD element is damaged.

G438 No outside help. Only the two DRIVERS may operate the ROBOT's controls during TELEOP. The DRIVE COACH and TECHNICIAN may advise but may touch the controls only to E-STOP, and no one else in the ALLIANCE STATION may touch the OPERATOR CONSOLE. No signals, controllers, or devices from anyone else. HUMAN PLAYERS feed game pieces by hand, with no tools. Violation: MAJOR FOUL, plus YELLOW CARD.

G439 Do not damage the FIELD. Penalties for FIELD damage are set by G412 alone. This rule adds that the ROBOT is DISABLED if the damage is a hazard or affects scoring. Replays follow T204. Violation: see G412.

G440 Keep the lanes open. A ROBOT may not sit in a side lane, between a PYLON and the guardrail, in a way that leaves no room for another ROBOT to pass, for more than 3 seconds. Violation: MINOR FOUL, repeated every 3 seconds.

Note: Defense is allowed and encouraged, especially against a ROBOT carrying a ROD across the field. Just stop before pinning (G405), and stay out of the TOWER ZONE in the ENDGAME (G416).

5.5 Post-MATCH

G501 (E) Leave the CELLS. DRIVE TEAMS must not remove CELLS from their ROBOT until the Head REFEREE has confirmed the score. Violation: VERBAL WARNING.

G502 (E) Score disputes. Only the DRIVE TEAM's student designee may ask the Head REFEREE about a ruling, and only before the next MATCH involving that team starts. Counting and sensor questions must be raised before the score is confirmed (4.5). After confirmation, the score changes only if the Head REFEREE finds a rules error in a FOUL, card or DISQUALIFICATION call. The Head REFEREE's rulings at an event are final.

R108 DrivetrainWheels or treads only; no legs, suction, adhesives or anything that marks or damages the carpet
R109 Starting volumeFits in a 38 in × 38 in box, 42 in tall, BUMPERS included

How it's measured. The FRAME PERIMETER is the string length around the ROBOT's frame at the BUMPER height, with BUMPERS off and mechanisms retracted. Extensions (R103, R107) are measured flat from that perimeter. Heights (R102, R106) are measured from the carpet to the highest point. The starting box (R109) includes BUMPERS and everything else. Game pieces a ROBOT CONTROLS count as part of the ROBOT for R103, R106, R107 and R1003.

Starting box test (R109). The ROBOT, in its starting configuration with BUMPERS on, must roll into an open-top 38 in × 38 in frame and fit under a 42 in bar without touching either. Curved or angled frame sides are measured the same way: the frame decides, not the drawing.

6.2 Safety and damage prevention

  • R201 (E) No sharp edges, pinch points, or parts likely to come loose and land on the FIELD.

  • R202 (E) No energy storage other than the battery, pneumatics and simple springs.

  • R203 Launchers must be enclosed enough that a CELL cannot be ejected above 12 ft off the carpet.

  • R204 No weapons-like parts. No wedges, spikes, sharp edges, or parts built to lift, tip, pierce, tangle, or grab other ROBOTS. Frame parts below the BUMPERS may not stick out past them.

  • R205 No hazardous materials. No mercury, flammable gas, open flame, explosives, or hydraulic fluid. No loose lithium batteries. Lithium cells are allowed only as sold inside small COTS electronics, such as cameras or controller backup cells; no power banks, and nothing that powers motors or actuators.

  • R206 Every configuration counts. Every way the ROBOT can be set up at an event must be inspected. The heaviest one is the weight that must meet the limit. Major part swaps (a new mechanism or drivetrain) need re-inspection before the next MATCH.

6.3 Budget and fabrication

  • R301 (E) No single part may cost more than $600, and total cost (excluding Kit of Parts items) may not exceed $5,500. Every mechanism a team uses at the event counts once, across all its configurations; identical spares in the pit don't. Kit of Parts items are free only up to the quantity in the Kit.

  • R302 (E) Fabricated parts must be made after Kickoff. Designs and code published before Kickoff may be reused.

  • R303 Each part counts at the price any team could pay a public vendor for it, even if it was donated, reused or bought on sale. A part is one item as sold; parts you assemble count separately. Shipping, tax and labor don't count. Teams keep a cost sheet in the pit for inspectors.

  • R304 How to price common cases. A kit sold as one item counts at the kit price. A pre-built mechanism bought assembled counts at its price, as one part. A team-made part counts at the cost of its raw material. Parts fabricated before Kickoff are not allowed (R302); COTS parts bought before Kickoff are fine and count at today's price. A part a sponsor makes counts at the price of the closest commercial equivalent, or its raw-material cost if nothing similar is sold, whichever is higher. Kit of Parts items are only those on the ORBITAL SURGE Kit of Parts checklist.

  • R305 Fabrication means cutting, machining, printing, bending or welding stock into a part. Assembling purchased parts is not fabrication, but a complete drivetrain or mechanism must be assembled after Kickoff.

6.4 BUMPERS

  • R401 (E) BUMPERS must cover at least 75% of the FRAME PERIMETER and sit between 2 in and 7 in off the carpet.

  • R402 Team numerals on the BUMPERS must be at least 4 in tall and contrast clearly with the bumper fabric.

  • R403 (E) BUMPER color must match the ALLIANCE: red or blue fabric, swapped between MATCHES.

  • R404 (E) BUMPERS are 3/4 in plywood backing, 5 in tall, faced with two rows of 2.5 in pool noodle under durable cloth, and attached with fasteners that hold during a hard hit but come off in under 5 minutes.

6.5 Motors, power and control

  • R501 (E) Only motors on the current FRC legal motor list may be used, with no limit on quantity, and may not be modified beyond mounting, connectors and gearing. Teams keep each motor's datasheet in the pit for inspectors.

  • R601 One 12 V, 17–18 Ah nonspillable sealed lead-acid battery, no heavier than 14 lb, secured so it survives a 6 in drop test at inspection.

  • R701 (E) The ROBOT must use exactly one ROBOT controller and one radio, both visible from outside the ROBOT.

  • R702 The ROBOT's status light must be visible from 3 ft away on at least two sides.

  • R703 (E) ROBOTS communicate only through the FMS network: no links between ROBOTS, and no other radios, Bluetooth or wireless devices. ROBOTS also may not signal each other with lights, sounds or displays meant to be read by another ROBOT.

6.6 OPERATOR CONSOLE

  • R901 (E) The OPERATOR CONSOLE must fit on the ALLIANCE STATION shelf, 60 in wide × 14 in deep, and may not use wireless communication or be connected to another team's OPERATOR CONSOLE in any way.

6.7 Launchers and game-piece mechanisms

  • R1001 A launcher may not send a CELL out faster than 40 ft/s (12.2 m/s). Inspectors fire 3 CELLS in every launcher mode at its highest setting; every shot must read 40 ft/s or less, with a 1 ft/s measurement tolerance. Code may not change launcher speed, piece limits or size based on whether the ROBOT is connected to the FMS, sees FIELD AprilTags, or is in a MATCH.

  • R1002 Mechanisms that grab RODS must hold them by the body or end caps only and may not crack the polycarbonate or the light core.

  • R1003 No part of a ROBOT may reach down into the CORE's rim opening more than 2 in below its top plane, or reach into an ARRAY slot more than 2 in past its front face.

6.8 Climbing mechanisms

  • R1101 Climbers may only touch a TETHER's cable sleeve and grab bar, never the TOWER truss.

  • R1102 A climber must lock on its own, with a ratchet, brake or latch, so the ROBOT stays up with power off for at least 5 minutes.

  • R1103 Hooks touching the TETHER must be smooth or rubber-lined, with no edge under a 1/8 in radius, so they cannot cut the sleeve.

  • R1104 FIELD STAFF must be able to lower a CLIMBED ROBOT by hand or with one tool-free release.

6.9 Pneumatics

  • R1201 (E) Air is stored at no more than 120 psi and used at no more than 60 psi, through a relief valve, a working-pressure regulator and visible gauges on both sides of the regulator.

  • R1202 (E) Use only rated pneumatic parts: off-the-shelf tanks, solenoids and cylinders marked for at least 125 psi.

  • R1203 (E) A manual vent valve must dump all stored air and be easy to reach.

6.10 Electrical

  • R1301 (E) Power runs through one 120 A main breaker that is easy to reach and press within 5 seconds.

  • R1302 (E) Every motor and controller sits on its own branch protected by a breaker of no more than 40 A.

  • R1303 (E) Wire is at least 6 AWG from the battery to the power hub, 12 AWG for 40 A branches and 18 AWG for 20 A branches or less.

  • R1304 (E) The ROBOT must stop all motion within 1 second of an E-STOP or lost radio link.

  • R1305 (E) No lasers above Class 1, no lights or lasers aimed at people, the FIELD or other ROBOTS' cameras, and no AprilTags or other fiducial markers on a ROBOT.

7 Inspection (I) and Tournaments (T)

A ROBOT must pass a safety check before any MATCH and a full inspection before its first qualification MATCH. Events run practice, qualification and double-elimination playoff MATCHES.

7.1 Inspection

  • I101 (E) Each ROBOT must pass a full inspection before its first qualification MATCH. Before playing a practice MATCH it must pass a safety check: BUMPERS, battery mounting and the E-STOP test (I107).

  • I102 (E) Inspectors weigh the ROBOT, check its size in the starting configuration, test the BUMPERS, and do the battery drop test (R601).

  • I103 Inspectors confirm the ROBOT cannot CONTROL a third CELL or a second ROD by trying to load them, and that no part is shaped to herd extra CELLS (a concave pocket wider than two CELLS).

  • I104 (E) A ROBOT changed significantly after inspection must be re-inspected before its next MATCH. Significant means a weight change over 5 lb (the ROBOT must still meet R104 at all times), a new or removed mechanism, a BUMPER change, or any change to starting size, launcher or climber.

  • I105 Inspectors clock the launcher with a radar gun or light gate at full power to confirm R1001.

  • I106 The ROBOT climbs a test TETHER under its own power, starting from the carpet, is powered off, and must stay up for 5 minutes (R1102). Inspectors record the highest LEVEL it reaches, which caps any climb credited by a FOUL (4.8). Inspectors then lower it with the release (R1104).

  • I107 (E) Inspectors trigger an E-STOP and pull the radio link while the ROBOT drives; it must stop within 1 second (R1304).

  • I108 Inspectors may re-check any ROBOT's launcher speed, size, weight and BUMPERS between MATCHES at any time during the event, in every software mode. Teams must give inspectors a way to run their MATCH code in the pits. A ROBOT that fails is DISABLED until it is fixed and passes again.

  • I109 Damage check. Inspectors may check any ROBOT that damaged an opponent before its next MATCH, and may require changes before it plays again.

  • I110 One ROBOT per team. Each team brings one ROBOT to an event. Spare parts are fine, but a second full ROBOT may not be swapped in.

7.2 Tournaments

  • T101 (E) Each team plays the same number of qualification MATCHES, 10–12, set by the event before it starts. A scheduling program assigns partners and opponents at random while spreading out repeats; when a team is needed to fill a slot it plays as a surrogate.

  • T102 (E) Teams are ranked by average Ranking Points over their scheduled MATCHES; surrogate MATCHES don't count. Ties break by average MATCH points (FOUL points included), then average TETHER points, then average AUTO points, then random draw.

  • T201 (E) The top 8 teams pick ALLIANCES of four teams, using a serpentine draft: round 1 picks go seed 1 to 8, round 2 goes 8 to 1, round 3 goes 1 to 8. A captain may pick any unpicked team, including a lower captain; a team may decline once and then can't be picked. When a captain is picked by another ALLIANCE, every lower captain moves up one seed and the highest-ranked unpicked team becomes the last captain. Events with fewer than 32 teams use one ALLIANCE per 4 teams, at least 2.

  • T202 (E) Playoffs are double-elimination among the ALLIANCES (8, or fewer under T201). Finals are best of three.

  • T203 Bonus RPs are not awarded in playoffs. Ties in playoffs break by FOUL points received (the ALLIANCE credited with more FOUL points wins), then TETHER points, then AUTO points, then a replay.

    T204 (E) Replays. A MATCH is replayed only for an ARENA fault (an FMS, sensor or FIELD element failure) that the Head REFEREE judges changed which ALLIANCE won or which RP it earned. ROBOT failures, battery problems and team radio issues are never replayed. Sensor errors referees can fix under 4.5 are fixed, not replayed. Differences within the ±1 in FIELD tolerance (3.1) are never grounds for a replay. An ARENA fault a ROBOT caused never earns that ROBOT's ALLIANCE a replay.

    T205 Four-team ALLIANCES. Before each playoff MATCH, an ALLIANCE picks which 3 of its 4 ROBOTS play. It may swap between MATCHES, including during its timeout, but never during a MATCH. Playoff cards apply to all 4 teams.

    T206 (E) Which MATCHES count. FOULS and cards are not assessed in practice MATCHES, but a ROBOT may still be DISABLED for safety. Cards from qualifications don't carry into playoffs. A card for conduct off the FIELD (G201, T402) applies to the next qualification or playoff MATCH the team actually plays. If none is left at the event, it counts against the team's last MATCH for ranking and awards.

    T207 (E) Not ready. A ROBOT that isn't on the FIELD and ready when the FIELD is ready is bypassed: it scores nothing and its team gets 0 RP for that MATCH, while its partners keep theirs. A playoff ALLIANCE that can't field 3 ROBOTS plays with fewer.

    T208 Repair time. If a ROBOT was damaged by an opponent's G409 or G434 foul, and the Head REF confirms the foul was called, its team may get up to 6 minutes of extra repair time before its next MATCH. A team may receive T208 time once per event in qualifications and once in playoffs. A team that claims damage that did not come from an opponent's foul gets no extra time.

  • T301 (E) Each ALLIANCE in playoffs gets one 6-minute timeout. It may only be called between MATCHES, before the next MATCH's countdown starts, never during a MATCH.

7.3 Pits and conduct

T401 Pit safety. Safety glasses must be worn in the pits and near the FIELD. No running. Batteries are charged only with proper chargers, away from walkways. A powered ROBOT in the pits must be up on a stand with its wheels off the floor.

T402 Respect other teams' ROBOTS. Damaging another team's ROBOT on purpose anywhere off the FIELD earns a RED CARD, applied as in T206, and may remove the team from the event.

T403 Cards carry forward. A YELLOW CARD carries through qualifications. A second YELLOW CARD becomes a RED CARD. Cards reset when playoffs begin (T206). A team removed from the event under T402 or G435 stays removed.
